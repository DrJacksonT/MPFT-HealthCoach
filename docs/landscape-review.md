# Current landscape and research gap

Checked 12 August 2026.

## What has already been done

Digital smoking interventions are established. NICE NG183 covers digital and mobile behaviour-change interventions, including stopping smoking: [NICE NG183](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng183). Conversational smoking interventions are not new. A systematic review and meta-analysis found a developing evidence base for conversational systems but substantial variation in study quality and methods: [Bendotti et al., Digital Health](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10623979/).

QuitBot has an expert-curated conversational programme covering motivation, quit dates, cessation medicines, triggers and relapse. A full-scale randomised trial protocol compares it with SmokefreeTXT: [Bricker et al., 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1551714424003100). Technology-assisted motivational-interviewing chatbots are also under active trial: [TAMI protocol](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1551714425003052). A 2025 systematic review covers natural-language chatbot interventions for diet, activity and smoking: [JMIR review](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2025/1/e66403).

Holly Health offers broad digital health coaching for long-term conditions and wellbeing and reports NHS partnerships: [Holly Health](https://www.hollyhealth.io/). Its existence means broad AI or digital health coaching cannot be claimed as novel. The NHS also provides a national Quit Smoking app and extensive fixed digital information.

## What may be distinctive

The plausible differentiation is narrower than "AI smoking coach". It is the product-level combination of:

- a guided programme rather than open chat;
- visible claim provenance and evidence lifecycle status;
- individual relevance without claiming individual prediction;
- progressive explanation of effect measures, certainty and limitations;
- a coach that can cite only an approved runtime library;
- an evaluation design capable of separating evidence translation from coaching.

This is a hypothesis about design differentiation, not proof of novelty or benefit. A formal review with registered search methods would be needed for a novelty claim.

## Unsafe novelty claims

Do not claim that this is:

- the first smoking cessation chatbot;
- the first use of generative language models for cessation;
- the first automated motivational-interviewing system;
- a clinically validated coach;
- proven to improve quit rates;
- the first personalised smoking intervention;
- unique across commercial products whose technical evidence architecture is not public.

## Genuine research gap

A useful research question is whether transparent, uncertainty-aware evidence relevance improves comprehension and decision quality, and whether adding constrained coaching changes motivation or behaviour beyond that information component. Existing conversational work often focuses on engagement, therapeutic alliance or cessation outcomes. This prototype can make claim provenance, limits of applicability and the information-versus-coaching mechanism explicit and measurable.

## Product implication

The evidence page remains authoritative without model generation. The free-text coach is secondary and removable. This keeps the intervention useful if the conversational component does not improve outcomes or cannot meet safety requirements.
