# Research concept

## Proposed question

Does an evidence-grounded personalised digital smoking coach improve understanding, motivation and smoking-related behaviour compared with standard digital smoking information?

## Intervention

Adults who smoke complete a short structured review. Normal application code selects approved population evidence using broad applicability tags. The participant sees why each item was selected, the source population, effect measure where useful, certainty, limitations and why it is not an individual prediction. They select a goal and complete brief check-ins. A separate optional component provides constrained, evidence-retrieving coaching for motivation, cravings, plans and setbacks.

## Rationale and mechanism

The proposed mechanism has four linked steps:

1. relevance draws attention to information that connects with an existing concern;
2. layered explanation improves comprehension of evidence and uncertainty;
3. autonomy-supportive dialogue helps the person state their own reasons and barriers;
4. a small chosen action and feedback convert intention into planning and learning.

Each step can fail. Relevant information can feel intrusive, evidence detail can overload, automated coaching can be shallow, and self-report can misrepresent behaviour. The evaluation should measure intermediate mechanisms rather than infer them from engagement.

## Comparator and study options

The simplest first comparison is the intervention against a current, fixed NHS information package. A later three-arm study could compare:

- standard information;
- personalised evidence translation;
- personalised evidence translation plus scoped coaching.

This would help separate the contribution of evidence relevance from conversation. A factorial design is possible but not necessary for an early feasibility study.

## Outcomes

### Primary feasibility and comprehension

- recruitment, eligibility and onboarding completion;
- comprehension of relative versus absolute effects;
- recognition that population evidence is not a personal prediction;
- understanding of benefits, limitations and uncertainty;
- usability and acceptability, including cognitive interviews.

### Motivation and behaviour

- importance, confidence and intention to attempt quitting;
- goal selection and attempts;
- cigarettes per day and quit attempts;
- self-reported days abstinent and point-prevalence abstinence at a prespecified follow-up;
- engagement with professional cessation support;
- objective validation such as carbon monoxide only in a later protocol with suitable governance.

### Safety and equity

- symptom, pregnancy, crisis and personal medicine queries;
- successful scope refusals;
- unsupported claims, uncited claims and false precision;
- drop-off and outcomes across eligible, offered, started, engaged, completed and benefited stages;
- usability for lower digital confidence, disability, language needs and shared-device contexts.

## Analysis principles

Predefine outcomes, follow-up, missing-data handling and the abstinence definition. Do not treat absent check-ins as smoking or abstinence. Separate intention-to-treat behaviour outcomes from product engagement. Report confidence intervals and absolute numbers. Analyse safety events at conversation and participant levels without retaining more free text than necessary.

## Safety and governance

The prototype is not ready for participants. A pilot requires study classification, sponsorship, proportionate ethics and R&D decisions, a clinical safety officer and safety case, DPIA, approved hosting and retention, incident handling, participant information, withdrawal/deletion rules, evidence change control and a current medical-device assessment.

## Implementation questions

- Can staff maintain claim-level evidence provenance without excessive burden?
- Which explanation depth is used, and by whom?
- Does relevance improve comprehension or mainly engagement?
- Does a free-text coach add value beyond well-designed structured prompts?
- Which groups are excluded by device access, literacy, language or trust?
- Can local stop-smoking services absorb referrals generated by the tool?

## Conference or publication angle

A defensible early contribution would be the intervention model, evidence-governance architecture, usability work and a feasibility comparison. It would be premature to claim a new treatment, a validated motivational-interviewing intervention, an exact personalisation engine, or the first smoking chatbot.
